Dizziness and Vertigo in Rahim Yar Khan: When the Room Spins

Dizziness and vertigo are common, and they are often confused with each other. Dizziness is a vague feeling of being light-headed or unsteady. Vertigo is more specific: it is the feeling that the room is spinning, or that you are spinning, even when you are standing still. Both can be frightening, and both can have many causes, so the cause has to be found before the right treatment can start.

Most cases of vertigo come from the inner ear, which controls balance. A common cause is a small crystal in the inner ear moving out of place, which brings on spinning when the head moves in a certain way. Other causes are low blood pressure, dehydration, low blood sugar, anaemia, the side effects of medicines, and migraine, which can cause vertigo even without a headache. Sometimes the cause is in the brain or the nerves, and that is when a neuro physician needs to be involved.

There are warning signs that need an urgent check. Vertigo with double vision, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side of the body, a severe headache, fainting, or vertigo that does not stop are all signs that the cause may be in the brain, such as a stroke. These should never be ignored, and the person should be seen the same day.

At a consultation, the neuro physician asks when the spinning started, what brings it on, and what other symptoms come with it. The examination checks balance, eye movements and the nerves, because the pattern of eye movements often shows whether the problem is in the ear or in the brain. Tests such as NCS or EEG are ordered only when they are needed. Treatment depends on the cause: some inner-ear problems are treated with simple head movements done in the clinic, some need medicines, and some need the underlying cause to be treated.
